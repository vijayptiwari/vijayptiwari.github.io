Wearable Technology and Extended Applications

An overview of computing that moves from devices we carry to systems we wear—continuous, contextual, and embedded in everyday activity.

Saurabh Marne · Harshal Patil · Vijay Prakash Tiwari · A. M. Ingole

WEARABLE COMPUTING / HCI24/7

Paper focus

From occasional interaction to continuous computing

The paper surveys wearable technology as the next step beyond handheld mobile devices: sensors, displays, and communication components carried on the body and available during normal activity. It frames wearables as an interface layer that can support real-time, contextual interaction.

Application areas include health and fitness, communication, navigation, entertainment, work, and safety. The review also considers the engineering limits that determine whether a device is genuinely wearable: power, comfort, size, connectivity, reliability, privacy, and cost.

Key themes

Designing for a device that stays with the user

  • Wearables reduce the distance between sensing, computation, and the user's immediate context.
  • Always-available interfaces can make interaction faster but also increase interruption and privacy risk.
  • Usefulness depends on battery life, ergonomics, data quality, connectivity, and social acceptance.
  • The strongest applications fit the form factor instead of reproducing a phone interface on a smaller screen.

Reading it today

An early map of a category now embedded in daily life

Published before smartwatches and health wearables reached their current maturity, the paper captures the category's shift from experimental gadgets toward a persistent computing platform. Many predicted application areas became common, while privacy, battery, and interoperability remain active constraints.

Claim boundary

The publication is a technology overview. It does not report a clinical study, a new wearable prototype, or comparative performance measurements; application benefits should be evaluated within their specific domain.
