Augmented Reality and Its Technologies

A survey of the display, sensing, interaction, and three-dimensional registration technologies that combine digital content with a real environment.

Vikas Tiwari · Vijay Prakash Tiwari · Dhruvesh Chudasama · Kumkum Bala

Paper focus

Three systems must agree: display, interaction, and registration

The paper surveys augmented reality as a real-time combination of physical surroundings and computer-generated content. It organizes the enabling technology into display systems, tracking or gesture-recognition systems, and three-dimensional modelling and registration.

It covers head-worn, handheld, and spatial displays; optical and video see-through approaches; interaction inputs; and the alignment problem that makes a virtual object appear anchored to the physical world.

Key themes

Augmentation is a systems-integration problem

  • Displays determine how real and virtual views are combined and where the user experiences them.
  • Tracking supplies position and orientation so digital content can respond in real time.
  • Three-dimensional registration preserves spatial alignment between virtual and physical objects.
  • Calibration, latency, field of view, portability, robustness, and natural interaction constrain the experience.

Reading it today

A useful decomposition that predates today's spatial-computing platforms

The hardware has advanced substantially since 2016, but the paper's decomposition remains recognizable: sensing, tracking, rendering, display, and interaction must work together within tight latency and calibration budgets.

Claim boundary

The paper is a literature survey, not a controlled evaluation of an AR device. Statements about future multisensory interaction are prospective and should be separated from capabilities demonstrated in the publication.
