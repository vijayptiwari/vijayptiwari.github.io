Face Analysis for Improvised System Security using LASER Focus

A conceptual enhancement to facial biometrics that uses focus and depth cues to distinguish a live three-dimensional subject from a flat photograph.

Paper focus

Recognition alone does not establish liveness

The paper starts from a practical weakness in basic camera-based facial recognition: a system may match the face shown in a photograph without establishing that a live person is present. It reviews face detection and recognition, then proposes adding laser-focus behavior as a physical-depth signal.

The proposed check relies on the difference between focusing across the varying contours of a three-dimensional face and focusing on a flat printed surface. In contemporary terminology, the goal is presentation-attack or spoof resistance.

Key themes

Layering identity matching with a physical-presence signal

  • Face detection locates a face; face recognition compares identity features; neither alone guarantees liveness.
  • A flat photograph and a live face produce different depth profiles for an active optical sensor.
  • Security improves when independent signals fail differently and must agree before access is granted.
  • Usability, eye safety, sensor accuracy, environmental performance, and adversarial testing are essential design constraints.

Verified correction

The journal record is Volume 3, Issue 4

The supplied description identified Issue 2, but the original PDF and the IRJET issue index both place this paper in Volume 3, Issue 4, April 2016. This archive uses the journal record.

Claim boundary

The paper proposes a security concept; it does not establish modern biometric liveness-detection performance. Production use would require controlled false-accept and false-reject testing, attack evaluation, privacy safeguards, and conformance with laser-safety requirements.
