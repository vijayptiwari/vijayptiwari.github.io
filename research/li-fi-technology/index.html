Li-Fi Technology, Implementations and Applications

A survey of data carried by visible light—from LED intensity changes to receiver design, modulation, use cases, and physical constraints.

Yash Khare · Vijay Prakash Tiwari · Akshay B. Patil · Kumkum Bala

When illumination becomes a data channelIRJET · Volume 3 · Issue 4 · pp. 1391–1394

Paper focus

Using LED light for wireless data transmission

The paper introduces Li-Fi through visible-light communication: an LED transmitter changes intensity rapidly enough to encode data while appearing continuously illuminated to the human eye, and a photodiode receiver recovers the signal.

It surveys the system structure, modulation approaches, practical applications, and trade-offs against radio-based communication. The physical inability of visible light to pass through opaque walls appears as both a coverage limitation and a possible containment advantage.

Key themes

Communication shaped by the medium

  • LEDs can perform two roles at once: illumination and high-frequency signal modulation.
  • Visible spectrum offers an alternative where radio-frequency congestion or restrictions matter.
  • Line of sight, shadowing, ambient light, and wall boundaries fundamentally shape system design.
  • Candidate settings include indoor connectivity, aircraft cabins, hospitals, and localized data delivery.

Reading it today

A technology survey from Li-Fi's early public adoption curve

The work captures the excitement around visible-light communication in 2016 and organizes the components required to reason about an implementation. Several performance statements in the field were still future-facing and dependent on laboratory conditions.

Claim boundary

This archive treats the paper as a survey, not evidence that Li-Fi universally outperforms Wi-Fi. Real deployments require environment-specific tests of throughput, mobility, coverage, interference, cost, and interoperability.
